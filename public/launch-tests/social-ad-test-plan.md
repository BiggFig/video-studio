# Three mobile ad concepts: audience testing brief

These are original creative hypotheses for Linear, Tally and Todoist. They are not official brand campaigns, and no spend, audience experiment or conversion result is represented as completed.

## What changed

- A specific recognizable problem opens each film.
- One piece of information persists through the product action and its result.
- Fast interruption gives way to a deliberate action, then a readable outcome.
- Separate 1080 × 1920 and 1080 × 1350 compositions replace landscape-first delivery. These are native layouts, not crops.
- The brand palettes and prior owned soundtracks remain. This round changes the visual story; it does not establish new audio effectiveness.

## The hypotheses

| Product | Audience / moment | Hypothesis | Product activation to measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linear | Software teams passing a bug to an AI coding agent | Keeping issue context through delegation makes the draft-PR outcome more relevant than a generic bug question | A qualified workspace or trial that completes a relevant first workflow; define available instrumentation with the brand |
| Tally | Freelancers collecting client briefs | Recognizing scattered enquiry details makes a simple form more desirable | A new user publishes a form; evaluate subsequent genuine submissions separately |
| Todoist | People remembering work after closing a laptop | Capturing one thought and seeing its date and priority persist communicates both the mechanism and the relief | A new user creates and schedules a task; assess return usage separately |

## Before audience delivery

1. Confirm product claims, brand approval, destination offer and music rights for the intended organic/paid usage. The included demonstrations are illustrative reconstructions.
2. Watch each video muted at actual phone size. Ask a viewer unfamiliar with the project: Who is it for? What happened? What changed? What should I do next? Record their words before explaining the concept. We have not yet conducted this external comprehension test.
3. Preview the selected file in each placement. Keep critical copy and product proof clear of the platform's controls and captions. The authored Reels safety area is conservative (x80–1000, y290–1230 at 1080 × 1920); confirm against the actual placement preview.
4. Verify the landing page repeats the promise and CTA. Agree on what a qualified sign-up and useful product activation mean before testing.

## Organic test

Post a placement-specific edition with the relevant product caption. Compare with the account's own comparable product posts, accounting for audience size, timing and distribution. Examine early retention and completion, but also saves/shares, relevant comments, profile or site visits and downstream activation when attributable. A single organic post cannot isolate a causal effect; use repeated observations and audience feedback.

## Paid test

This brief does not launch a campaign or authorize an unspecified budget.

- Test the new concept against the brand's existing control using the platform's randomized experiment where available. Keep audience, placement, landing page, offer, optimization objective and attribution window consistent. Set an acceptable acquisition cost, sample requirement and stopping rule from the account's baseline before launch.
- Treat the current comparison with our previous landscape cuts as a package comparison: message, motion and format all changed. It cannot establish that the hook alone caused a result.
- For a subsequent hook-only test, keep the selected new body, CTA, format, audio and delivery settings fixed; change only the first two seconds. Those alternate exports are not included in this release.
- Separate cold acquisition from warm retargeting. Do not pool results from the two audiences and call the creative a winner.
- Report exposure, spend and uncertainty with the results. Do not select a winner just because the platform happened to deliver more impressions to it.

## Read the whole funnel

| Signal | What it helps diagnose | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Early video retention | Whether the opening holds attention | Purchase intent or incremental sales |
| Completion / watch time | Whether the story sustains viewing | That the viewer understood or valued it |
| Outbound click rate | Whether the promise earns investigation | A qualified customer |
| Landing-page visits / sign-ups | Continuity from creative to destination | Product activation or retained value |
| Cost per qualified activation | Acquisition against the agreed goal | Long-term retention or incremental lift by itself |

If attention is weak, inspect opening relevance and readability. If attention is strong but click intent is weak, inspect the consequence and CTA. If clicks arrive without activation, inspect audience fit and the destination experience rather than blindly adding more animation. Scale only after evidence meets the brand's agreed economics and confidence requirements.
