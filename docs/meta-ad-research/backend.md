# Software ad frame study — backend contribution

Observed 2026-10-03. Six distinct videos across ClickUp, Notion and monday.com. Research uses actual decoded archive media, not the archive's generated summaries. All 540 half-second samples across 26 contact sheets were visually inspected. No audio review or real-time playback is claimed. Scene boundaries are approximate within 0.5 seconds.

## Provenance and limits

These six records are **archive evidence**. A separate live Meta visit resolved the ClickUp advertiser page and showed other active ClickUp creatives; it did not verify the exact six selected creatives or their library IDs. CDN path numbers are not promoted to verified Meta IDs. Motion's status labels do not establish current delivery. No performance result is inferred. All advertising prices, offers and savings remain unverified creative claims.

Whole videos and full contact sheets remain in ignored `.local/meta-references/backend`. Published JPEGs contain six small timestamped excerpts per video solely to support criticism and scene analysis. Published descriptions are original paraphrases; no archive AI summaries are reproduced.

**Hook:** A rapidly increasing cost figure above a presenter turns a familiar software-stack problem into a concrete consequence.

| Approximate time | What is visible | Motion / purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5.5s | **Count the consequence.** Presenter at a desk under a large recurring-cost counter; short supporting captions. | Number increments and caption replacements; presenter framing remains a stable anchor. Establish an economic pain before naming the alternative. |
| 5.5–13s | **Make fragmentation countable.** Tool names stack above the presenter, followed by login and invoice counts. | Quick punch-ins, stacked typography and brief paper-like fragments. Translate an abstract fragmented stack into tangible administrative friction. |

| 22.5–31.5s | **Replace claims with interface cards.** Task status, document table, whiteboard, chat and clip-menu views appear over the host. | Individual proof cards slide and replace one another in a consistent screen region. Show recognizable evidence for a broad feature proposition. |
| 31.5–42.5s | **Continue the product inventory.** Notetaker/transcript, task creation, scheduling grid and AI-answer card. | Successive UI replacements with tight framing on each example. Sustain proof cadence without relocating the presenter anchor. |
| 42.5–50s | **Compress the proposition.** Forms and time tracking conclude the UI sequence; short consolidation statements return. | Brief color wash, hard type substitutions and a closer presenter shot. Reassemble the many examples into a single proposition. |
| 50–61.5s | **Price comparison.** A paid-plan card and cost comparison occupy the upper composition. | Large numbers, plan-card entrance and counter changes. Supply the ad's financial rationale; these figures are not independently verified. |
| 61.5–67.072s | **Direct call to action.** Presenter, large start-oriented type and website ending. | Stable closing composition after the rapid proof sequence. Make the next action easy to identify. |

**Mechanism:** Quantify one problem, then answer it through a regular sequence of small interface proofs. The method is useful; the advertised savings are not transferable evidence.

**Application:** Open Linear with one recognizable work blockage rather than a generic product introduction. Keep the issue/composer/PR as recurring physical objects instead of borrowing the presenter, colors or financial claims.

**Hook:** A colleague asks for a link; alternating reactions turn a small familiar request into a coordination problem.

| Approximate time | What is visible | Motion / purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | **A recognizable question.** Office performer asks another colleague for a link; the reverse view is behind a large monitor. | Immediate angle reversal with simple captions. Invite recognition before explaining the product. |

| 14.5–21s | **Name the fragmented problem.** Presenter gestures through several places where work can be scattered. | Caption replacement follows the gestural enumeration. Connect the sketch to an understandable software need. |
| 21–25s | **Show the workspace.** Phone-camera view of a ClickUp task table on a monitor. | Short pan across the actual workspace. Provide concrete product context after the sketch. |

| 36.5–40.309s | **Brand end card.** White background, ClickUp logo and workspace-start call to action. | Logo entrance followed by a readable end hold. Separate the brand action from the comic setup. |

**Mechanism:** A tiny recognizable question creates tension before any product explanation. Alternating cause/reaction shots make a simple premise move.

**Application:** Use an original developer question on the actual issue object: Bug reported. Now what? Answer through the documented delegation and draft PR, rather than adding unrelated jokes or unverified speed claims.
