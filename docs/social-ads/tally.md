# Tally — one client brief, one place

Original art-directed product-ad study, 2026-10-05. Two independently composed 18-second exports: 1080×1920 Reels and 1080×1350 feed. Neither is a crop of the landscape film. This is a creative hypothesis, not a performance result or official Tally campaign.

## Audience and promise

Freelancers and small studios collecting prospective client details through scattered messages. The specific benefit is a structured project enquiry containing a name, contact address and project request. The video does not promise more leads, conversion gains, saved hours, automatic qualification, or integration with messaging platforms.

Opening: **“Still taking client briefs in DMs?”** An audience can recognize the workflow before they recognize the product. A clear question and recognizable fragments establish a problem; the next scene supplies the alternative. This replaces the previous generic “Stop guessing. Start asking.”

## Sources checked

- [Create a form](https://tally.so/help/create-a-form): text-document style editor, slash insertion, Publish, and sharing a form link. Enlarged chrome follows the white/ink/gray official editor with blue Publish.
- [Input blocks](https://tally.so/help/input-blocks): short answer, email and long answer fields used in the example. No unsupported field or automated parser.
- [Thank-you pages](https://tally.so/help/how-to-create-a-thank-you-page): default post-submit screen and branding; the prior source pack holds its screenshot.
- [Question fields](https://tally.so/help/how-to-name-fields): questions correspond to retained answers. We use an editorial recap to keep all three answers legible instead of inventing a mobile submissions dashboard.
- [FAQ](https://tally.so/help/faq): forms and submissions available for free, subject to Tally’s terms. The film makes the narrower “Create a free form” offer.

Source inspection on 2026-10-05. Ada and the example.com address are illustrative. No real client information, real submission, messaging account, publish action, or campaign is involved. The Tally symbol comes from the existing official-source study. Typography is approximated with the bundled Geist font. UI content is HTML/SVG, not screenshots.

## Placement and creative treatment

Reels essential content fits x80–1000 and y290–1230, leaving conservative quiet space for surrounding app controls. Feed has its own header, hook, message, form, result and CTA positions inside x80–1000/y110–1200. The working form is 880 pixels wide in both, question labels at 38px, input answers at 40px, and main headlines at 76px or larger. No transformation shrinks key fields below their designed readable scale.

The visual contrast moves from incomplete scattered messages to precise field construction to a large functional button, then typing, confirmation and a calm complete brief. It uses motion to show tension and resolution, not a new color palette. Music can remain the previous owned soundtrack so it cannot compensate for unclear visuals.

## Proposed distribution copy

Organic caption: “A client brief shouldn’t be scattered across messages. Give your next enquiry a place to land: name, email, project details. Build a free form with Tally.”

Paid primary copy: “Still collecting client details one message at a time? Build a project enquiry form with Tally. Ask for a name, email and project details in one place. Create a free form.”

Paid headline: “Your next client brief, in one form.”

Suggested CTA: “Sign Up”, pointing to the actual Tally form-creation journey if the brand approves the destination. These are proposed creative assets only; no ad account or campaign was changed.

## What to test

Compare this use-case hook against the prior generic hook with audience, destination, placement and attribution held constant. Reels and feed should be separate placement reads. Judge initial attention and click-through together with qualified form creation and activation, not views alone. The expected improvement is clearer audience recognition and a concrete reason to try the product. It remains unproven until tested with traffic.
